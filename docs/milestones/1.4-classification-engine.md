# Milestone 1.4 — Classification engine v1

**Status:** Complete

**Completed:** 2026-07-01

**Pull request:** [#8 Implement classification engine v1](https://github.com/vijendharv/USCollegeSelection/pull/8)

## Objective

Classify a first-year applicant's fit for one institution as Safety / Likely, Target, Reach, or Insufficient Data using deterministic, versioned, explainable rules. Missing or incompatible evidence must reduce confidence rather than become favorable evidence.

## Delivered

### Versioned rule engine

`classify_admission` is a pure function over a student profile, institution, dataset version, and optional institution admissions benchmark. Methodology version `1.0` uses these boundaries:

- an acceptance rate at or below 20% forces Reach, even for students above published ranges;
- a value below a compatible published academic range produces Reach;
- Safety / Likely requires an acceptance rate of at least 50% and all compatible academic signals above their published upper bounds;
- supported comparisons without a Reach or Safety / Likely condition produce Target; and
- no compatible academic comparison produces Insufficient Data unless the highly selective override applies.

The constants live in application code and are covered at their exact boundaries. A language model cannot silently change them.

### Compatible academic evidence

The engine compares:

- cumulative GPA only when student and institution benchmark have the same weighted/unweighted type and numerical scale;
- total SAT against the sum of published Evidence-Based Reading/Writing and Math 25th/75th percentiles; and
- ACT composite against published composite 25th/75th percentiles.

SAT and ACT are optional; either test alone can support classification. If multiple totals for one test are present, the highest is used. If both SAT and ACT are supplied, the stronger comparison against that school's published range is used and the weaker comparison is disclosed as excluded. Section scores are not mistaken for totals. When the student chooses not to submit tests, SAT and ACT are excluded from classification rather than reported as missing.

### Course rigor kept separate

Confirmed Honors, AP, IB, and dual-enrollment courses produce a descriptive rigor rule. They do not add points to GPA or change category because the initial data does not yet include a comparable institution rigor benchmark.

### Explainable end-user result

Every result includes:

- category and low, medium, or high confidence;
- methodology version;
- triggered rules with student values and school ranges;
- source dates and URLs;
- a plain-language explanation;
- `missing_inputs`, rendered to the user as **Missing data**; and
- `excluded_factors` for evidence that was present but incompatible or intentionally unused.

This distinction prevents a weighted GPA on a 5.0 scale from being mislabeled as absent when the school publishes only an unweighted 4.0 range.

## Design decisions

- Keep v1 conservative: acceptance rate can force Reach but cannot by itself manufacture a Safety.
- Treat the published 25th-to-75th interval as the typical range and require a score strictly above the upper endpoint for Safety / Likely evidence.
- Preserve weighted and unweighted GPA semantics instead of converting school-reported values.
- Keep missing data and excluded evidence separate in the user-facing contract.
- Avoid major-specific, residency, direct-admit, and holistic adjustments until comparable sourced inputs exist.
- Keep classification independent of storage and networking so it is fast and testable offline.

## How to use it

```python
from app.classification import classify_admission

result = classify_admission(student, institution, dataset_version, admissions_benchmark)

print(result.category)
print(result.confidence)
print(result.missing_inputs)  # Display as "Missing data"
```

## Deferred
