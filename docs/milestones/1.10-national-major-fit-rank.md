# Milestone 1.10 — National major fit rank and admissions benchmarks

**Status:** Complete

**Implementation pull request:** [#16](https://github.com/vijendharv/USCollegeSelection/pull/16)

## Objective

Separate a nationwide, student-specific major fit position from the existing fit rank inside each admissions category. Improve admissions benchmark visibility and keep student-supplied colleges distinct from generated recommendations.

## Delivered behavior

- Every exact six-digit CIP program match receives both a deterministic national student-major fit rank and a separate national program-strength rank with comparison populations.
- National ranking includes all eligible US institutions analyzed, not only the schools selected for the final category-balanced shortlist.
- Ranking is ordered first by evidence confidence, then by the versioned student fit score and stable UNITID tie-breaker.
- The category rank is relabeled `Recommendation Rank Within Classification` because qualified results are ordered by program strength before student fit.
- A college-major combination must have an exact six-digit CIP match and score at least 80/100 to enter the qualified pool.
- Generated recommendations take the ten strongest programs from the qualified pool inside each admissions category; selection is deterministic and never random or alphabetical.
- Remaining qualified colleges are exported as a JSON collection, final Excel worksheet, and PDF addendum.
- National rank is explicitly described as an internal student-fit result, not a published or commercial prestige ranking.
- The College List displays the reported ACT composite 25th-to-75th percentile range.
- A sourced compatible admitted-student GPA range is displayed when present; otherwise the report says it is unavailable from the current official dataset.
- The repeated missing-data message no longer implies that a required GPA download failed.
- Student-supplied colleges appear in a separate first Excel worksheet and a separate PDF table before generated recommendations.
- Supplied names not found in the current federal institution universe remain visible as unresolved rather than disappearing silently.

## Methodology and tradeoffs

The national student-fit position answers “where does this school rank for this student and this major?” The separate program-strength position uses only major completions, field outcomes, and institution outcomes; it excludes the student's academic standing, preferences, and résumé. Neither claims to be a commercial prestige ranking. Schools without exact six-digit IPEDS bachelor-program evidence are not assigned a national ordinal because broader CIP families are too ambiguous for that claim.

The main recommendation table first requires a student-fit score of at least 80, then orders qualified colleges by program-strength evidence before using student fit as a tie-breaker. This prevents a one-point fit difference from placing a much weaker program ahead of a substantially stronger one. Categories with fewer than ten qualified colleges are not padded. Qualified results after the first ten are retained in the addendum.

Confidence is sorted before numeric fit score so a sparse-data score cannot outrank a well-supported score merely because missing weights were redistributed. The report exposes both the ordinal and population, such as `50 of 612`, and retains all component scores and missing inputs.

College Scorecard supplies ACT percentile data but does not supply an admitted-student high-school GPA distribution. IPEDS admissions data identifies how GPA is considered but does not provide a broadly comparable admitted-student GPA range in the current pipeline. The app therefore reports GPA benchmarks only when separately sourced and compatible by weighted/unweighted type and scale.
